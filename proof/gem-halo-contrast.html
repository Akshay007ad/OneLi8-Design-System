Gem content halo, measured

The quiet Gem face is clear glass (neutral-030 at 0.18 environmental, 0.27 stabilized), so it cannot guarantee what sits behind the content. The stated mechanism is that the content carries its own contrast via material.gem.color.quiet.contentGlow at radius material.gem.blur.contentGlow. This page measures whether that actually holds.

Method: the text is drawn to a canvas twice — once with the halo, once without — and the halo's alpha is read from the pixels that carry no ink. Adjacent is the mean alpha in the 1px ring touching the glyph stems, which is the background WCAG 1.4.3 compares the ink against. Peak is the densest halo pixel anywhere near the glyph, which flatters the result. The plate is the warm end of a bright daylight scene (#FDD68C) — the worst case for near-white ink, and the case the light appearance has to survive. CSS and canvas both use sigma = radius / 2.

The result: a wider halo is a weaker halo. Blurring spreads a fixed alpha budget over more area, so peak density falls as radius grows — a thin 12px stem convolved with a large sigma produces a fringe, not a scrim. Stacking the same shadow accumulates alpha instead of spreading it, and is the only single change here that moves the number materially.
